§1 · The Cognitive Stress Test
Section1
The Neurosteer Assessment

A cognitive stress test for the brain.

Just as a resting ECG can miss ischemia that appears only under exercise, a resting EEG misses the cognitive reserve deficits that emerge under demand.

A single disposable forehead electrode. Audio-guided tasks. A response clicker. Twenty-five minutes to a complete brain health profile.

01 · Setup
Single-electrode setup
One disposable hdrEEG™ electrode strip on the forehead (Fp1–Fp2). No gel, no cap, no preparation. Bluetooth streaming to tablet. Ready in under two minutes.
02 · Tasks
Audio-guided cognitive tasks
The patient listens to musical and verbal cues — "Click when you hear the violin" — and responds with a hand clicker. Tasks escalate from simple detection through demanding 3-back working memory.
03 · Analysis
AI pipeline analysis
A proprietary transformer neural network — trained on 10,000+ EEG sessions — extracts 16 deep-learning neuromarkers in real time, reflecting brain performance under each cognitive load level.
04 · Report
Instant assessment report
Age-stratified normative scoring, Executive Function Age, flagged neuromarkers with clinical interpretations, and a personalized neurofeedback protocol.

§3 · Neural Effort (NE)
Section3
Neural Performance Efficiency Profile

NE — Neural Effort.

NE measures how much neural resource the brain deploys at each cognitive load level. Lower NE = more efficient processing.

Elevated NE reflects compensatory over-recruitment — the brain working harder than necessary — strongly associated with cognitive decline (MMSE r = −0.44 at high load, d = +1.44 vs Mild Dementia).

NEAt Rest
Neural efficiency at rest
MMSE r = −0.34
Healthy brain shows low NE at rest — DMN disengaging cleanly. Elevated resting NE indicates chronic compensatory over-recruitment.
⚠ If elevatedImpaired DMN disengagement. Assess for anxiety, sleep disruption, or early decline.
NFB resting NE reduction · sleep assessment · mindfulness.
NEModerate Load
Resource use at moderate demand
MMSE r = −0.34
NE should increase moderately under 1-back tasks. If elevated at rest AND mid-load, the brain has no efficiency reserve left.
⚠ If elevatedCognitive strain under typical demands — reaching reserve limit.
NFB targeting NE reduction under moderate load.

Efficiency under peak demand
MMSE r = −0.44 · d = +1.44
Clinical sign: disproportionate elevation — the brain working harder but accomplishing less. Strongest MMSE correlate (r = −0.44).

§4 · TBR & BAR
Section4
Attention & Stress Reactivity Profile

TBR & BAR — attention and stress.

TBR measures attentional regulation — dual-sided: elevated (poor control) and very low (neurodegenerative disruption) are both flagged.

BAR measures cortical stress reactivity at high load — the only Neurosteer neuromarker directly validated against salivary cortisol (r = −0.42) and strongest MMSE correlate (r = −0.50).

TBRLow Load
Theta / Beta balance
MMSE r = +0.48 · d = −0.93
In healthy attentive brains, beta dominates during cognitive tasks. Theta dominance signals low-focus, internally-directed state — reduced sustained engagement.
⚠ Dual-sided flagHIGH TBR: attentional dysregulation — ADHD / fatigue / chronic stress (d = −0.93 vs Dementia).
LOW TBR: neurodegenerative disruption — warrants neuropsychological evaluation.
High → TBR-reduction NFB. Low → formal MoCA & rule out dementia.
BARHigh Load
Beta / Alpha ratio · cortisol-validated
MMSE r = −0.50 · cortisol r = −0.42
BAR captures the alpha-dominant relaxation → beta-dominant arousal shift under cognitive challenge. Only Neurosteer neuromarker with direct endocrine validation — r = −0.42 with pre-session cortisol (N ≈ 40).
⚠ If elevatedCortisol-validated stress over-reactivity. Assess for anxiety disorder, chronic stress, or HPA-axis dysregulation.
BAR-reduction NFB · HRV biofeedback · stress-axis workup.

§4 · TBR & BAR — Scientific Background
Fig 4.3BAR and RT produce the largest group separations — both exceeding d = 1.0.

TBR neurophysiology. Theta (4–8 Hz) is generated by hippocampal-cortical circuits — among the first systems disrupted in neurodegeneration. Very low TBR in older adults reflects disrupted theta-generating circuits — validated as a marker of cognitive decline (MMSE r = +0.48).

BAR endocrine validation. Validated in a naturalistic outdoor setting (N = 101) where beta-dominated components were selectively activated by acute startle (Maimon et al., 2026). A neurological dissociation study (N = 68) confirmed Beta/Gamma activity is specifically elevated by stress but not cognitive demand alone.

§5 · CA & ME
Section5
Working Memory & Task Activation Profile

CA & ME — the dual-catecholamine profile.

CA (Cognitive Alertness) reflects noradrenergic cognitive alertness — the brain's rest-to-task engagement transition, absent in both Mild Dementia and F-DOPA+ Parkinson's.

ME (Memory Engagement) tracks the working memory network's ramp-up under increasing demand — an individual cognitive style marker with the strongest high-load cortisol correlation (r = +0.40).

CALow Load
Noradrenergic task-readiness
MMSE r = +0.23
Healthy brains show a clear rest → task increase (+4.23 units, p < 0.001). Absent activation is the hallmark — present in both Mild Dementia and F-DOPA+ Parkinson's — pointing to shared locus coeruleus neurodegeneration.
⚠ If below P5 (age-adjusted)LC-NE alertness deficit. In patients ≥ 65: neurological evaluation for early Parkinson's or prodromal dementia.
CA activation NFB · alertness engagement training.
MEHigh Load
WM network engagement
cortisol r = +0.40
ME measures WM network activity during demanding tasks. An individual cognitive fingerprint — not a group-discriminating marker. Stronger ME engagement predicts faster responses and higher post-session cortisol.
Individual markerMost valuable for longitudinal tracking — within-subject changes across sessions reveal WM-network response to NFB.

§6 · AS & SR — Physiological Stress
Section6
Physiological Stress Profile

AS & SR — physiological stress.

AS (Autonomic Stress) is the most physiologically grounded Neurosteer neuromarker — validated directly against HRV (PNN50 r = −0.34, SDNN r = −0.24, n = 75). It bridges EEG and cardiology.

SR (Stress Reactivity) captures the acute stress reaction during peak cognitive demand — a pure stress-reactivity index, dissociated from cognitive function.

ASAt Rest
HRV-validated resting stress
PNN50 r = −0.34 · SDNN r = −0.24
Higher AS = higher sympathetic arousal = lower HRV = reduced stress resilience. Does NOT discriminate cognitive groups (d = −0.15) — a pure autonomic stress index independent of cognitive function.
Informational metricAS does not contribute to the cognitive decline score. Elevated AS with normal cognitive metrics may reflect test anxiety or chronic stress.
AS NFB · HRV biofeedback (6 breaths/min) · sleep hygiene · GAD-7 if AS > 70.
SRHigh Load
Stress response under demand
MMSE r = −0.09 · d = +0.13
SR captures the transformer model's assessment of physiological stress during 2-back and 3-back tasks. Negatively correlates with self-reported calmness under high load.
Informational onlyElevated SR under high load = stress system over-responds during challenge. Dissociates from AS in the two-tier neural stress hierarchy.

§7 · RT & Error Rate
Section7
Behavioral Performance Profile

RT & Error Rate — the behavioral signal.

Behavioral performance during the hdrEEG™ assessment produces strong group separation across the cognitive-decline continuum.

RT at low load achieves d = 1.44 and MMSE r = −0.46. Patients press a response clicker during each task; accuracy and response time are recorded continuously and averaged per cognitive load level.

RTLow Load
Processing speed at baseline
MMSE r = −0.46 · d = +1.44
RT during simple digit-detection tasks reflects baseline neural processing speed. Slowing above 2500 ms indicates significant cognitive impairment.
⚠ If > P80 (2348 ms)Reduced processing speed (d = 1.44). Often co-occurs with elevated NE and reduced CA.
ErrorLow Load
Accuracy at baseline tasks
MMSE r = −0.387 · d = +1.24
Healthy adults make almost no errors on simple detection (mean 5.4%). Mild Dementia patients make errors on trivially easy tasks 24.8% of the time — a 5-fold increase.
⚠ If > P80 (5.9%)Attentional lapses during easy tasks — clinically significant. Rule out ADHD, fatigue, medication. Combine with NE and TBR to determine cause.

§12 · Value of Assessment
Section12
The Value of Assessment

The missing biomarker in every patient panel.

Comprehensive health panels include objective data for cardiovascular risk, metabolic health, and hormonal status. Brain health — which determines quality of life and independence — has had no equivalent objective assessment. The Neurosteer hdrEEG™ assessment changes that.

01

Objective brain biomarkers

16 quantified neuromarkers, validated against MMSE, HRV, and salivary cortisol — the same rigor as a blood panel, applied to the brain.

02

Earlier signal than standard tests

Reserve deficits at mid- and high-load are invisible on resting EEG — detectable years before functional decline appears on standard assessments.

03

Personalized treatment targets

The report pinpoints exactly which metrics are out of range and generates a targeted neurofeedback protocol unique to that cognitive profile.

04

Trackable over time

Repeat every 3–6 months. Executive Function Age and neuromarker scores provide longitudinal data — the same framework as VO₂ max, now for the brain.

05

Integrates with existing data

AS bridges EEG and HRV. Executive Function Age complements epigenetic clocks. NE and BAR cortisol coupling links EEG to the endocrine panel.

06

Quality of life is cognitive

Physical health extends years. Cognitive health determines how those years are lived — engagement with family, profession, and purpose.

When to assess

Four inflection points.

Initial evaluationBaseline — benchmark cognitive profile before any intervention.
After 10 NFB sessionsMid-treatment — quantify neuromarker trajectory.
Full protocol completionOutcome — pre/post comparison on all 14 scored metrics.
