Section 1 · The Assessment
The Neurosteer Assessment

A cognitive stress test for the brain.

Just as a resting ECG misses ischemia under exercise — a resting EEG misses the cognitive reserve deficits that emerge under demand.

01 · Setup
Single electrode
One disposable hdrEEG™ strip on the forehead (Fp1–Fp2). No gel, no cap. Ready in under 2 minutes.
02 · Tasks
Audio-guided tasks
Musical and verbal cues escalate from simple digit detection through demanding 3-back working memory. Response clicker.
03 · Analysis
AI pipeline
Transformer network trained on 10,000+ sessions extracts 16 brain metrics in real time, per cognitive load level.
04 · Report
Instant report
Age-stratified scoring, Executive Function Age, flagged metrics with interpretations, and a personalized NFB protocol.

Section 3 · Neuromarker: Neural Effort (NE)
Neural Performance Efficiency Profile

Neural Effort (NE) — Lower = more efficient processing.

NEAt Rest
Neural efficiency at rest
MMSE r = −0.34 · d = +0.91
Healthy brains show low NE at rest — the default-mode network disengages cleanly. Elevated resting NE = chronic compensatory over-recruitment.
⚠ Impaired DMN disengagement · assess for anxiety, sleep disruption, or early cognitive decline
NFB resting NE reduction · sleep assessment · mindfulness protocols
NEModerate Load
Resource use at moderate demand
MMSE r = −0.34 · d = +1.27
NE should increase moderately under 1-back — appropriate mobilization. Elevated at rest AND moderate load = brain has no efficiency reserve remaining.
⚠ Cognitive strain at moderate demand · reaching reserve limit early
NFB targeting NE reduction under moderate load
NEHigh Load
Efficiency under peak demand
MMSE r = −0.44 · d = +1.44 ★
Some NE rise under 2-back/3-back is healthy. The clinical sign: disproportionate elevation — the brain working harder but accomplishing less. Strongest MMSE correlate.
⚠ Cognitive reserve failure under peak demand — largest effect size in battery (d = +1.44)
High-load NFB protocol · serial 3-month reassessment

Section 4 · Neuromarkers: TBR · BAR
Attention & Stress Reactivity Profile

Theta-Beta Ratio · Beta-Alpha Ratio.

TBRLow Load
Theta / Beta balance · attention regulation
MMSE r = +0.48 · d = −0.93
In healthy attentive brains, beta dominates during cognitive tasks. Theta dominance signals a low-focus, internally-directed state. Dual-sided flag: both high (attentional dysregulation) and very low (neurodegenerative disruption) are clinically significant.
⚠ HIGH TBR: ADHD / fatigue / chronic stress (d = −0.93 vs Dementia)
⚠ LOW TBR: neurodegenerative disruption of theta-generating circuits
High → TBR-reduction NFB · attention training · Low → formal MoCA, rule out dementia
BARHigh Load
Beta / Alpha ratio · cortisol-validated
MMSE r = −0.50 ★ · cortisol r = −0.42
Captures the alpha→beta shift under cognitive challenge. The only Neurosteer metric directly validated against salivary cortisol (r = −0.42, N ≈ 40). Strongest MMSE correlate in the entire battery (r = −0.50).
⚠ Cortisol-validated stress over-reactivity · assess for anxiety disorder, HPA-axis dysregulation
BAR-reduction NFB · HRV biofeedback · stress-axis endocrine workup

Section 5 · Neuromarkers: CA · ME
Working Memory & Task Activation Profile

Cognitive Alertness · Memory Engagement.

CALow Load
Noradrenergic task-readiness
MMSE r = +0.23
Healthy brains show a clear rest→task increase (+4.23 units, p < .001). Absent activation is present in both Mild Dementia AND F-DOPA+ Parkinson's — pointing to shared locus coeruleus neurodegeneration.
⚠ LC-NE alertness deficit · age ≥ 65: evaluate for early Parkinson's or prodromal dementia
CA activation NFB · alertness engagement training
MEHigh Load
WM network engagement
cortisol r = +0.40 (strongest high-load)
Measures working memory network activity during 2-back/3-back tasks. An individual cognitive fingerprint — not a group-discriminating marker. Stronger ME predicts faster responses and higher post-session cortisol.
Individual marker — most valuable for longitudinal tracking of WM-network response to NFB

WM mobilization capacity
individual profile
The ramp-up from low to high demand. Flat ΔME signals limited working-memory mobilization capacity — a trainable target for within-session NFB protocols.

Section 6 · Neuromarkers: AS · SR
Physiological Stress Profile

Autonomic Stress · Stress Reactivity.

ASAt Rest
HRV-validated resting stress
PNN50 r = −0.34 · SDNN r = −0.24 · N = 75
Validated directly against HRV — bridges EEG and cardiology in one session. Higher AS = higher sympathetic arousal = lower HRV = reduced stress resilience. Does NOT discriminate cognitive groups (d = −0.15).
Pure autonomic stress index — independent of cognitive function. Elevated AS with normal cognitive metrics = test anxiety or chronic stress, not decline.
AS NFB · HRV biofeedback (6 breaths/min) · sleep hygiene · GAD-7 if AS > 70
SRHigh Load
Stress response under demand
MMSE r = −0.09 · d = +0.13 (informational)
Captures the acute stress reaction during 2-back and 3-back tasks. Negatively correlated with self-reported calmness under high load. Pure stress reactivity marker — dissociated from cognitive function.
Informational only — not included in the composite score. Elevated SR = stress system over-responds during peak cognitive challenge.

Section 11 · Neurofeedback Plan
AI-Personalized Neurofeedback Protocol

AI-personalized neurofeedback plan.

The patient watches a movie. The Neurosteer system monitors the personalized combined neuromarker in real time, modulating screen brightness and audio volume — passive for the patient, active for the brain. No task engagement required.

Block 1 · Cognitive Training · 10 Sessions
Combined neuromarker: NE + TBR

Goal. Reduce NE toward age-adjusted norm — same cognitive performance with fewer neural resources. Normalize TBR toward the healthy attention-regulation range.

Expected outcomes. NE typically decreases 10–20% after 10 sessions. TBR normalization correlates with improved sustained attention, focus, and social engagement.

Structure. 30–40 min · 2–3× / week · real-time audio + video modulation.

Targets. NE Rest → below P80 = 75.4 · TBR → reduce toward P80 = 7.1

Block 2 · Stress & Autonomic Training · 10 Sessions
Combined neuromarker: AS + BAR

Goal. Reduce resting autonomic stress (AS) and normalize stress reactivity (BAR). Brain learns parasympathetic tone through real-time reinforcement.

Expected outcomes. AS reduction 15–25% after 10 sessions. BAR normalization correlates with lower reported anxiety and improved stress resilience.

Complementary. Breathwork (6 breaths/min resonance frequency) · sleep hygiene · consider HPA-axis endocrine workup.

Targets. AS Rest → target P70 · BAR High → below P80

Reassess after 10 sessions and at protocol completion · Repeat full assessment every 3–6 months for longitudinal monitoring.
