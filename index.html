Section1
The Neurosteer Assessment

A cognitive stress test for the brain.

Just as a resting ECG can miss ischemia that appears only under exercise, a resting EEG misses the cognitive reserve deficits that emerge under demand.

The Neurosteer hdrEEG™ assessment is the neurological equivalent of a cardiac stress test — systematically escalating cognitive load while monitoring brain and behavioral responses at every step. A single disposable forehead electrode. Audio-guided tasks. A response clicker. Twenty-five minutes to a complete brain health profile.

01 · Setup
Single-electrode setup
One disposable hdrEEG™ electrode strip on the forehead (Fp1–Fp2). No gel, no cap, no preparation. Bluetooth streaming to tablet. Ready in under two minutes.
02 · Tasks
Audio-guided cognitive tasks
The patient listens to musical and verbal cues — "Click when you hear the violin" — and responds with a hand clicker. Tasks escalate from simple detection through demanding 3-back working memory.
03 · Analysis
AI pipeline analysis
A proprietary transformer neural network — trained on 10,000+ EEG sessions — extracts 16 deep-learning neuromarkers in real time, reflecting brain performance under each cognitive load level.
04 · Report
Instant assessment report
Age-stratified normative scoring, Executive Function Age, flagged neuromarkers with clinical interpretations, and a personalized neurofeedback protocol — automatically generated for the consultation.

Four load levels, one session: Rest (eyes closed, music, meditation) → Low (digit detection, 0-back) → Mid (1-back, sentences) → High (2-back, 3-back, delayed recall). This escalating design reveals the reserve deficit — the point at which compensatory mechanisms begin to fail — that resting EEG cannot detect.

Cognitive profile

Executive Function Age.

AI-derived r = 0.85 MAE 13.2 yr

Model. Bias-corrected ElasticNet regression · 20 behavioral + neural features · Production v2-0-1 · Validated on N = 363 healthy subjects.

Comprehensive health panels track biological age across multiple systems — epigenetic clocks, telomere length, arterial stiffness. Executive Function Age is the brain's equivalent.

An AI-derived estimate of how old the brain is performing today, based on 20 behavioral and neural features from the assessment. Sensitive to today's cognitive state — tiredness, stress, medications — making it a powerful tool for tracking treatment response over time.

Fig 2.1Healthy patient example — Executive Function Age within expected range.

Longitudinal use. Because behavioral features dominate the model, Executive Function Age responds to neurofeedback. Serial assessments every 3–6 months quantify whether treatment is reducing the gap — objective evidence of brain health improvement beyond self-report.

Section3
Neural Performance Efficiency Profile

NE — Neural Effort.

NE measures how much neural resource the brain deploys at each cognitive load level. Lower NE = more efficient processing.

Elevated NE at rest is the EEG signature of compensatory over-recruitment — the brain working harder than necessary — a pattern strongly associated with cognitive decline (MMSE r = −0.44 at high load, d = +1.44 vs Mild Dementia). An elevated NE today reflects a brain already working at its reserve limit.

NE At Rest
Neural efficiency at rest
MMSE r = −0.34
A healthy brain shows low NE at rest — the default-mode network is disengaging cleanly, conserving resources. Elevated resting NE indicates chronic compensatory over-recruitment.
⚠ If elevated Impaired DMN disengagement — chronic cortical over-recruitment. Assess for anxiety, sleep disruption, or early cognitive decline.
NFB resting NE reduction · sleep assessment · mindfulness protocols.
NE Moderate Load
Efficiency at moderate load
MMSE r = −0.34
NE should increase moderately under 1-back and sentence tasks — appropriate mobilization. If elevated at rest AND mid-load, the brain has no efficiency reserve left.
⚠ If elevated Cognitive strain under typical task demands. Compensatory hyperactivation — reaching reserve limit at low difficulty.
NFB targeting NE reduction under moderate load.
NE High Load
Efficiency under peak demand
MMSE r = −0.44 · d = +1.44
Some NE rise under 2-back / 3-back is healthy and expected. The clinical sign: disproportionate elevation — the brain working harder but accomplishing less. Strongest MMSE correlate (r = −0.44).
⚠ If disproportionately elevated Cognitive reserve failure under demand. Largest effect size in the battery — d = +1.44 vs Mild Dementia.
High-load NFB protocol · serial 3-month reassessment.

Section4
Attention & Stress Reactivity Profile

TBR & BAR — attention and stress.

TBR measures attentional regulation — top-down focus during cognitive tasks. A dual-sided marker: both elevated (poor control) and very low (neurodegenerative disruption) are flagged.

BAR measures cortical stress reactivity at high load — the only Neurosteer neuromarker directly validated against salivary cortisol (r = −0.42) and the strongest MMSE correlate of all 16 metrics (r = −0.50).

TBR Low Load
Theta / Beta balance
MMSE r = +0.48 · d = −0.93
In healthy attentive brains, beta dominates during cognitive tasks. Theta dominance signals a low-focus, internally-directed state — reduced capacity for sustained engagement in conversation, work, and social situations.
⚠ Dual-sided flag HIGH TBR: attentional dysregulation — ADHD / fatigue / chronic stress (d = −0.93 vs Dementia).
LOW TBR: neurodegenerative disruption of theta-generating circuits — warrants neuropsychological evaluation.
High → TBR-reduction NFB. Low → formal MoCA & rule out dementia.
BAR High Load
Beta / Alpha ratio · cortisol-validated
MMSE r = −0.50 · cortisol r = −0.42
BAR captures the shift from alpha-dominant relaxation to beta-dominant arousal under cognitive challenge. The only Neurosteer neuromarker with direct endocrine validation — r = −0.42 with pre-session salivary cortisol (N ≈ 40).
⚠ If elevated Cortisol-validated stress over-reactivity. Assess for anxiety disorder, chronic stress, or HPA-axis dysregulation.
BAR-reduction NFB · HRV biofeedback · stress-axis workup.
Scientific background TBR & BAR — evidence base
Fig 4.3BAR and RT produce the largest group separations — both exceeding d = 1.0, a very large effect.

TBR neurophysiology. Theta (4–8 Hz) is generated by hippocampal-cortical circuits — among the first systems disrupted in neurodegeneration. Very low TBR in older adults reflects disrupted theta-generating circuits — validated as a marker of cognitive decline (MMSE r = +0.48, p < 0.001).

BAR endocrine validation. Validated in a naturalistic outdoor setting (N = 101) where beta-dominated components were selectively activated by acute startle (Maimon et al., 2026). A neurological dissociation study (N = 68) confirmed Beta/Gamma activity is specifically elevated by stress but not cognitive demand alone.

Working Memory & Task Activation Profile

CA & ME — the dual-catecholamine profile.

CA (Cognitive Alertness) reflects noradrenergic cognitive alertness — the brain's rest-to-task engagement transition, absent in both Mild Dementia and F-DOPA+ Parkinson's.

ME (Memory Engagement) tracks the working memory network's ramp-up under increasing demand — an individual cognitive style marker with the strongest high-load cortisol correlation of all metrics (r = +0.40). Together: the dual-catecholamine cognitive engagement profile.

CA Low Load
Noradrenergic task-readiness
MMSE r = +0.23
Healthy brains show a clear rest → task increase (+4.23 units, p < 0.001). Absent activation is the hallmark clinical sign — present in both Mild Dementia and F-DOPA+ Parkinson's — pointing to shared locus coeruleus neurodegeneration.
⚠ If below P5 (age-adjusted) LC-NE alertness deficit. In patients ≥ 65: neurological evaluation for early Parkinson's or prodromal dementia.
CA activation NFB · alertness engagement training.
ME High Load
WM network engagement
cortisol r = +0.40
ME measures WM network activity during demanding tasks. An individual cognitive fingerprint — not a group-discriminating marker. Stronger ME engagement predicts faster responses and higher post-session cortisol.
Individual marker Most valuable for longitudinal tracking — within-subject changes across sessions reveal WM-network response to NFB.
ME Rest-to-Task Δ
WM mobilization (high − low)

The ramp-up from low to high demand. Flat ΔME signals limited working-memory mobilization capacity — a trainable target for within-session NFB protocols.

Physiological Stress Profile

AS & SR — physiological stress.

AS (Autonomic Stress) is the most physiologically grounded Neurosteer neuromarker — validated directly against HRV (PNN50 r = −0.34, SDNN r = −0.24, n = 75). It bridges EEG and cardiology in one 25-minute assessment.

SR (Stress Reactivity) captures the acute stress reaction during peak cognitive demand — a pure stress-reactivity index, dissociated from cognitive function.

AS At Rest
HRV-validated resting stress
PNN50 r = −0.34 · SDNN r = −0.24
Higher AS = higher sympathetic arousal = lower HRV = reduced stress resilience. Does NOT discriminate cognitive groups (d = −0.15) — a pure autonomic stress index independent of cognitive function.
Informational metric AS does not contribute to the cognitive decline score. Elevated AS with normal cognitive metrics may reflect test anxiety or chronic stress — not decline.
AS NFB · HRV biofeedback (6 breaths/min) · sleep hygiene · GAD-7 if AS > 70.
SR High Load
Stress response under demand
MMSE r = −0.09 · d = +0.13
SR captures the transformer model's assessment of physiological stress during 2-back and 3-back tasks. Negatively correlates with self-reported calmness under high load. Pure stress reactivity — dissociated from cognitive function.
Informational only Elevated SR under high load = stress system over-responds during challenge. Dissociates from AS in the two-tier neural stress hierarchy.

Section7
Behavioral Performance Profile

RT & Error Rate — the behavioral signal.

Behavioral performance during the hdrEEG™ assessment produces strong group separation across the cognitive-decline continuum.

RT at low load achieves d = 1.44 and MMSE r = −0.46. Patients press a response clicker during each task; accuracy and response time are recorded continuously and averaged per cognitive load level.

RT Low Load
Processing speed at baseline
MMSE r = −0.46 · d = +1.44
RT during simple digit-detection tasks reflects baseline neural processing speed. Slowing above 2500 ms indicates significant cognitive impairment.
⚠ If > P80 (2348 ms) Reduced processing speed (d = 1.44). Often co-occurs with elevated NE and reduced CA.
Error Low Load
Accuracy at baseline tasks
MMSE r = −0.387 · d = +1.24
Healthy adults make almost no errors on simple detection (mean 5.4%). Mild Dementia patients make errors on these trivially easy tasks 24.8% of the time — a 5-fold increase.
⚠ If > P80 (5.9%) Attentional lapses during easy tasks — clinically significant. Rule out ADHD, fatigue, medication. Always combined with NE and TBR to determine cause.
RT High Load
Processing speed under working memory demand
d = +0.96
Response speed during the most demanding working memory tasks (2-back, 3-back). A large increase from low load RT may indicate processing difficulty under cognitive challenge.
⚠ If > P80 (age-adjusted ~2300 ms) Reduced processing speed under peak demand. Often co-occurs with elevated NE and low ME engagement. Compare to low-load RT to assess load-dependent slowing.
Error High Load
Accuracy under peak cognitive demand
d = +1.23
Error rate on the most difficult working memory tasks. Some increase from low load is normal — healthy adults average 23.1% at high load. Elevated values reflect working-memory capacity failure.
⚠ If > P80 (age-adjusted ~40%) Excessive errors under peak demand — working-memory capacity constraint. Combine with ME engagement level and NE load profile to distinguish capacity failure from inattention.

10.3 Clinical pattern — compensatory overactivation

Pattern 1: High NE + High BAR + High TBR. The brain is over-recruited (NE), under stress (BAR), and poorly focused (TBR). Executive Function Age gap of +14 years confirms accelerated brain aging.

This profile — typical of MCI and early dementia — reflects a neural system sustaining performance through compensatory mechanisms that are becoming unsustainable. The patient's social engagement, professional performance, and physical independence are all at risk as reserve erodes.

Monitoring & reassessment The hdrEEG™ assessment can be repeated every 3–6 months to objectively track treatment response. Executive Function Age and neuromarker scores provide longitudinal data for the individual patient.

11.1 How Neurosteer neurofeedback works

The patient watches a movie or TV episode. The Neurosteer system monitors the personalized combined neuromarker in real time and modulates screen brightness and audio volume continuously — when brain activity moves toward the healthy range, the viewing experience is optimal; deviations dim the image and reduce volume.

This passive-for-the-patient, active-for-the-brain operant conditioning is engaging and well-tolerated, particularly by older adults and patients who find traditional active-task NFB fatiguing. No active participation beyond watching the content.

Block 1 · Cognitive training — 10 sessions
Combined neuromarker: NE + TBR
NE Rest · Atypical TBR Low · Atypical

Goal. Reduce NE toward age-adjusted norm — the same cognitive performance with fewer neural resources. Normalize TBR toward the healthy attention-regulation range.

Session structure. 30–40 min · 2–3× / week · real-time audio + video modulation · no active participation required.

Expected outcomes. NE typically decreases 10–20% after 10 sessions. TBR normalization correlates with improved sustained attention, focus, and complex social engagement.

The Value of Assessment

The missing biomarker in every patient panel.

Comprehensive health panels now include precise, objective data for cardiovascular risk, metabolic health, hormonal status, and genetic predisposition. Brain health — the domain that ultimately determines quality of life, independence, and capacity to benefit from everything else — has had no equivalent objective assessment.

The Neurosteer hdrEEG™ assessment changes that.

01

Objective brain biomarkers

16 quantified neuromarkers, validated against MMSE, HRV, and salivary cortisol — the same objective rigor as a blood panel, applied to the brain.

02

Earlier signal than standard tests

Reserve deficits visible at mid- and high-load are invisible on resting EEG — detectable years before functional decline appears on standard assessments.

03

Personalized treatment targets

The report pinpoints exactly which metrics are out of range and generates a targeted neurofeedback protocol unique to that cognitive profile.

04

Trackable over time

Repeat every 3–6 months. Executive Function Age and neuromarker scores provide longitudinal data — the same framework as VO₂ max or epigenetic age, now for the brain.

05

Integrates with existing data

AS bridges EEG and HRV. Executive Function Age complements epigenetic clocks. NE and BAR cortisol coupling links EEG directly to the endocrine panel.

06

Quality of life is cognitive

Physical health extends years. Cognitive health determines how those years are lived — engagement with family, profession, and purpose.

When to assess

Initial evaluationBaseline · benchmark cognitive profile before any intervention.
After 10 NFB sessionsMid-treatment · quantify neuromarker trajectory.
Full protocol completionOutcome · pre/post comparison on all 14 scored metrics.
