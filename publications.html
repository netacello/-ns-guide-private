  • Ref [1]
    Clinical implementation of single EEG channel for assessment of cognitive function in geriatric patients

    Molcho, L., Maimon, N. B., Regev-Plotnik, N., Rabinowicz, S., Intrator, N., & Sasson, A. (2022). Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience, 14, 773692.

    Journal Peer Reviewed doi:10.3389/fnagi.2022.773692

    Background: Cognitive decline remains highly underdiagnosed despite efforts to find novel cognitive biomarkers. Electroencephalography (EEG) features based on machine-learning (ML) may offer a non-invasive, low-cost approach for identifying cognitive decline. However, most studies use cumbersome multi-electrode systems. This study aims to evaluate the ability to assess cognitive states using machine learning (ML)-based EEG features extracted from a single-channel EEG with an auditory cognitive assessment.

    Methods: This study included data collected from senior participants in different cognitive states (60) and healthy controls (22), performing an auditory cognitive assessment while being recorded with a single-channel EEG. Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) scores were used to designate groups, with cutoff scores of 24 and 27. EEG data processing included wavelet-packet decomposition and ML to extract EEG features.

    Results: MMSE scores significantly correlated with reaction times and EEG features A0 and ST4. The features also showed significant separation between study groups: A0 separated between the MMSE < 24 and MMSE ≥ 28 groups, in addition to separating between young participants and senior groups. ST4 differentiated between the MMSE < 24 group and all other groups, showing sensitivity to subtle changes in cognitive states. EEG features Theta, Delta, A0, and VC9 showed increased activity with higher cognitive load levels, present only in the healthy young group.

    Discussion: This study successfully demonstrated the ability to assess cognitive states with an easy-to-use single-channel EEG using an auditory cognitive assessment. The short set-up time and novel ML features enable objective and easy assessment of cognitive states.

  • Ref [2]
    Automated frontal single-channel EEG cognitive screening: Systematic evaluation across two cohorts

    Molcho, L., Maimon, N. B., Zeimer, T., Chibotero, O., Rabinowicz, S., Armoni, V., Bar On, N., Intrator, N., & Sasson, A. (2025). Scientific Reports.

    Journal Peer Reviewed doi:10.1038/s41598-025-10983-2

    Timely detection of cognitive decline is paramount for effective intervention, prompting researchers to leverage EEG pattern analysis, focusing particularly on cognitive load, to establish reliable markers for early detection and intervention. This comprehensive report presents findings from two studies and a meta-analysis, involving a total of 237 senior participants, aimed at investigating cognitive function in aging populations.

    In the first study, 80 seniors were classified into two groups: 40 healthy individuals (MMSE > 28) and 40 at risk of cognitive impairment (MMSE 24–27). Dimensionality reduction models achieved a sensitivity of 0.90 and a specificity of 0.57, indicating a robust capability for detecting cognitive decline.

    The second study involved 77 seniors divided into three groups: healthy (MMSE > 27), at risk of MCI (MMSE 24–27), and mild dementia (MMSE < 24). Results demonstrated significant differences between MMSE groups and cognitive load levels, particularly for Gamma band and A0. A meta-analysis including 237 seniors and 112 young controls identified significant associations between EEG biomarkers such as A0 and cognitive assessment scores including MMSE and MoCA.

    The study highlights the feasibility of using a single-channel EEG device combined with advanced machine learning models, offering a practical and accessible method for evaluating cognitive function in various settings.

  • Ref [3]
    Evaluation of Parkinson's disease early diagnosis using single-channel EEG features and auditory cognitive assessment

    Molcho, L., Maimon, N. B., Hezi, N., Zeimer, T., Intrator, N., & Gurevich, T. (2023). Frontiers in Neurology, 14, 1273458.

    Journal Peer Reviewed doi:10.3389/fneur.2023.1273458

    Background: Parkinson's disease (PD) often presents with subtle early signs, making diagnosis difficult. F-DOPA PET imaging provides a reliable measure of dopaminergic function and is a primary tool for early PD diagnosis. This study aims to evaluate the ability of machine-learning (ML) extracted EEG features to predict F-DOPA results and distinguish between PD and non-PD patients.

    Methods: Participants comprised cognitively healthy patients who had undergone an F-DOPA PET scan as part of their standard care (n = 32) and cognitively healthy controls (n = 20). EEG data collected using the Neurosteer system during an auditory cognitive task was decomposed using wavelet-packet analysis and machine learning methods. A connectivity analysis was applied in a manner similar to fMRI connectivity to build a preliminary classification model.

    Results: The prediction model correctly classified patients with unrevealed scores as positive F-DOPA. EEG feature A0 and the Delta band revealed distinct activity patterns separating between study groups, with controls displaying higher activity than PD patients. In controls, EEG feature L1 showed variations between resting state and high-cognitive load — an effect lacking in PD patients.

    Conclusion: These findings exhibit the potential of single-channel EEG technology in combination with an auditory cognitive assessment to distinguish positive from negative F-DOPA PET scores, showing promise for early PD diagnosis.

  • Ref [5]
    Continuous monitoring of mental load during virtual simulator training for laparoscopic surgery reflects laparoscopic dexterity

    Maimon, N. B., Bez, M., Drobot, D., Molcho, L., Intrator, N., Kakiashvilli, E., & Bickel, A. (2022). Frontiers in Neuroscience, 15, 694010.

    Journal Peer Reviewed doi:10.3389/fnins.2021.694010

    Cognitive Load Theory (CLT) relates to the efficiency with which individuals manipulate the limited capacity of working memory. Repeated training generally results in individual performance increase and cognitive load decrease, as measured by both behavioral and neuroimaging methods. One known biomarker for cognitive load is frontal theta band, measured by EEG. Simulation-based training is an effective tool for acquiring practical skills, particularly for training surgeons in a controlled, hazard-free environment.

    In this study, 38 medical students and interns underwent three experiments examining their behavioral performance on a surgery simulator, while brain activity was monitored by a single-channel EEG. Each experiment included three trials with laparoscopic hands. The time retention between tasks was varied to examine changes during the task and as a result of overnight sleep consolidation.

    Participants' behavioral performance improved with trial repetition in all three experiments. Delta band and the novel VC9 biomarker exhibited a significant decrease in activity with trial repetition. Additionally, delta, VC9, and theta activity decreased with better individual performance.

    In correspondence with previous research, EEG markers delta, VC9, and theta (partially) decreased with lower cognitive load and higher performance. The novel biomarker VC9 showed higher sensitivity to lower cognitive load levels. These measurements may be used for neuroimaging assessment of cognitive load during laparoscopic tasks, potentially expanding to evaluation of different medical simulations and real surgical cognitive load.

  • Ref [4]
    Predicting working memory load from single frontal EEG channel

    Maimon, N. B., Lamy, D., Molcho, L., & Intrator, N. (2021). Proceedings of IEEE LifeTech 2021.

    Conference Peer Reviewed doi:10.1109/LIFETECH52111.2021.9391963

    Working Memory (WM) load is a cognitive feature highly correlated with mental effort. Theta power, among other neurological biomarkers, shows increased activity with increasing WM load. These correspondences frequently break down in individuals with cognitive decline, making WM load biomarkers a potentially valuable tool for early detection of cognitive impairment. However, such studies have relied on multiple-channel EEG headsets, which are not easy to use or accessible.

    In a single experiment, fourteen participants completed the widely used n-back task while brain activity was recorded with the Aurora by Neurosteer® single-channel EEG device. Frontal theta power, as well as two novel ML features, significantly correlated with levels of WM load. However, the two novel features exhibited higher sensitivity to lower WM load changes.

    These more sensitive biomarkers of WM load are a promising tool for mass screening of mild cognitive impairment.

  • Ref [6]
    EEG reactivity changes captured via mobile BCI device following tDCS intervention — a pilot-study in disorders of consciousness (DOC) patients

    Maimon, N. B., Intrator, N., Molcho, L., Barron, J., Jaul, E., & Meiron, O. (2022). Proceedings of IEEE BCI Conference 2022.

    Conference Peer Reviewed doi:10.1109/BCI53720.2022.9734839

    This pilot study examined open-label transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) treatment in six patients with disorders of consciousness (DOC), focusing on clinical benefits and changes in event-related EEG reactivity immediately following 10 days of anodal left-prefrontal cortex tDCS versus pre-treatment parameters, and clinical recovery over a one-year post-treatment period.

    Patient EEG was collected via the Neurosteer® system (mobile 3-electrode neurological BCI with cloud-based online analysis of classical EEG bands and novel machine-learning biomarkers) and a standard 19-channel clinical EEG. Machine-learning-based biomarkers and auditory event-related potentials showed greater sensitivity to neural changes than traditional clinical assessments. The mobile system captured data during routine CRS-R examinations at bedside without additional sessions or specialized personnel.

  • Ref [11]
    Detecting interruption events during cognitive work using single-channel frontal EEG

    Bolton, F., Toch, E., Te'Eni, D., & Maimon, N. B. (2021). Proceedings of IEEE LifeTech 2021.

    Conference Peer Reviewed doi:10.1109/LIFETECH52111.2021.9391915

    Contemporary computing devices subject their users to continuous interruptions that can seriously harm productivity and well-being. Understanding how people react to notifications can provide valuable information in managing undesirable interruptions.

    Participants in a lab experiment (n = 15) received notifications while carrying out a primary task, with brain activity recorded by a wearable EEG system. The study examined whether specific EEG features can distinguish between notifications that interrupt the user's activity and notifications that the user can disregard.

    Results demonstrate that wearable EEG can serve as a basis for managing interruptions, showing that specific EEG features can distinguish between interrupting and non-interrupting notification events.
